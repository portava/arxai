Product goal:
Create a full Ruby Flame Scalp Engine that can:

1. Detect scalp opportunities.
2. Separate scalp trades from longer trades.
3. Identify running flame momentum.
4. Measure flame quality, flame age, flame stage, and chase risk.
5. Determine whether the scalp is BUY, SELL, WAIT, or NO SCALP.
6. Identify whether the scalp is one-stick, short burst, momentum continuation, or multi-entry run-on flame.
7. Recommend when to enter, wait, add, hold, close latest, close partial, or close all.
8. Manage multiple scalp entries as one basket.
9. Adjust recommendations based on spread, execution speed, broker latency, bridge heartbeat, symbol personality, and current market structure.
10. Learn from scalp outcomes over time.

Important:
Ruby must never call something a scalp just because it is on a lower timeframe.
Ruby must never call a trade a scalp without answering “Why now?”
Ruby must not use swing/long-trade logic for scalp decisions.
Ruby must not keep stale scalp signals active after the move is gone.
Ruby must warn when a user is chasing.
Ruby must warn when the flame is exhausted.
Ruby must manage the trade after entry, not just find the entry.

Core definition:
A scalp is a short-duration momentum opportunity where price shows immediate directional pressure strong enough to move quickly over the next 1–5 candles, sometimes extending to several candles only if the running flame remains healthy.

Ruby should think:
“Is there a fast, tradable burst happening right now, and can the user enter, add, manage, or exit before the burst is over?”

Do not let Ruby think:
“Is this market generally bullish or bearish?”
That is long-trade logic, not scalp logic.

SECTION 3 — RUNNING FLAME DETECTION

Create Running Flame detection.

Running flame means candles are moving with immediate pressure in one direction.

Measure:

1. Consecutive same-direction candles.
2. Candle body size compared to recent average body size.
3. Candle body expansion or contraction.
4. Candle close position inside the candle range.
5. Wicks against the move.
6. Wicks in favor of the move.
7. Close near high for BUY flame.
8. Close near low for SELL flame.
9. Momentum acceleration.
10. Whether price is breaking structure or only moving inside chop.
11. Whether the last 1–5 candles show directional pressure.
12. Whether a pullback is controlled or becoming reversal.
13. Whether the current candle is continuing, stalling, or rejecting.
14. Whether the flame is early, active, late, or exhausted.

BUY flame signs:

1. Consecutive bullish candles.
2. Bodies expanding or staying strong.
3. Closes near candle highs.
4. Pullbacks shallow.
5. Lower wicks show rejection.
6. Price reclaiming or breaking micro resistance.
7. No major resistance immediately ahead.

SELL flame signs:

1. Consecutive bearish candles.
2. Bodies expanding or staying strong.
3. Closes near candle lows.
4. Pullbacks shallow.
5. Upper wicks show rejection.
6. Price rejecting or breaking micro support.
7. No major support immediately ahead.

Ruby must label flame state:

1. None
2. Igniting
3. Active
4. Run-on
5. Stretched
6. Weakening
7. Exhausted
8. Failed
9. Reversal risk

SECTION 4 — FLAME STAGE MODEL

Use this scalp flame cycle:

1. Ignition

* First strong candle or breakout is starting.
* First entry may be possible.
* Must still confirm “why now.”

2. Confirmation

* Second candle or retest confirms direction.
* Add-on may become possible.

3. Run-on Flame

* Multiple candles continue with strong closes.
* Multi-entry can be considered if exposure allows.
* Must watch for stretch and danger zones.

4. Stretch

* Move is still going but may be late.
* Stop adding by default.
* Protect profit.
* Only caution add allowed if rules permit.

5. Weakening

* Bodies shrink, wicks appear, pullbacks deepen.
* Close latest entry or partial may be recommended.
* No new add-ons.

6. Exhaustion

* Strong reversal wick, engulfing candle, failed continuation, or target/danger reached.
* Close all or avoid new entry.
* Signal expires.

7. Reversal

* Opposite pressure confirms.
* Original scalp invalid.
* Opposite scalp only allowed after new confirmation, not immediately by impulse.

Ruby must use flame stage to control:

1. Entry recommendation
2. Add-on permission
3. Hold/exit recommendation
4. Scalp signal freshness
5. Trade ticket warnings

SECTION 5 — SCALP FRESHNESS AND SIGNAL DECAY

Scalp signals must expire.

Track signal age:

1. Fresh = 0–1 candle old
2. Active = 1–3 candles old
3. Late = 4–6 candles old
4. Expired = 7+ candles old or after rejection/exhaustion

Add scalp confidence decay:

1. At signal: full score
2. After 1 weak/no-continuation candle: reduce score
3. After 2 no-continuation candles: downgrade to weak/wait
4. After reversal wick or failed breakout: invalidate
5. After target/danger zone reached: expire
6. After spread spike or stale bridge heartbeat: downgrade or block

Ruby must say:
“This was a valid scalp earlier, but it is now late.”
or
“Signal expired — wait for a new pullback/retest or new flame ignition.”

Do not keep stale scalp recommendations active.

“Why now?”

Valid “why now” reasons:

1. Flame just ignited.

6. Liquidity sweep snapped back.
7. Support failed with strong close.

If Ruby cannot produce a clear “why now,” then classification must be:

2. “Trend is down.”
3. “Indicators agree.”
4. “Could go higher.”
5. “Momentum exists.”

Those are not enough for scalp entry.

SECTION 12 — SCALP SETUP TYPES

Ruby must label scalp setup type:

1. Breakout scalp
2. Retest scalp
3. Continuation scalp
4. Rejection scalp
5. Reversal scalp
6. Exhaustion scalp
7. Liquidity sweep scalp
8. Failed breakout reversal scalp
9. Pullback scalp
10. No scalp / chop

Each setup type needs different confirmation.

Breakout scalp:

* Needs strong close through level.
* Avoid if candle is already too stretched.

Retest scalp:

* Needs broken level to hold.
* Better entry timing than chasing breakout.

Continuation scalp:

* Needs flame active and shallow pullback.
* Avoid if flame weakening.

Rejection scalp:

* Needs wick rejection and close away from danger.
* Target nearest liquidity/mean.

Liquidity sweep scalp:

* Needs sweep, rejection, snapback.
* Must be fast and cautious.

Exhaustion scalp:

* Opposite-direction scalp only after confirmation.
* Do not call reversal too early.

No scalp/chop:

* Mixed candles, overlapping candles, no clean target, spread too high, or fakeouts.

A one-stick scalp is a special high-speed scalp targeting one candle or one immediate burst.

Allow one-stick scalp only when:

1. Flame just ignited.

3. Spread is clean.
4. Execution speed is excellent.
5. Bridge heartbeat is fresh.
6. EA heartbeat is fresh.
7. Target is very nearby but still larger than cost.
8. No major danger zone is directly in front.
9. The user is not late/chasing.
10. “Why now” is clear.

One-stick scalp output:

1. Type: One-stick momentum scalp
2. Direction: BUY/SELL/WAIT
3. Expected window: 1 candle
4. Entry trigger
5. Quick target
6. Fast invalidation
7. Execution quality
8. Exit urgency

If execution quality is bad, block one-stick scalp recommendation.

SECTION 14 — MULTI-TRADE SCALP / RUN-ON FLAME

Add Multi-Trade Scalp logic.

A multi-trade scalp is when a run-on flame is strong enough for multiple manual entries within one fast momentum move.

Ruby must not blindly recommend adding. Every add-on needs its own score and reason.

Multi-entry status:

1. Allowed
2. Caution
3. Do Not Add
4. Close/Manage Only

Add-on permission levels:

1. Level 0 = no add allowed
2. Level 1 = add only on pullback/retest
3. Level 2 = add on continuation confirmation
4. Level 3 = strong run-on flame, multiple adds allowed within risk

Allow add-ons only when:

1. Original scalp score is strong enough, usually 75+.
2. Flame stage is Igniting, Confirmation, Active, or Run-on.
3. Entry timing is Early, Clean, or Acceptable.
4. Add-on timing is not Chasing.
5. Price has not reached target/danger zone.
6. Spread/execution remains clean.
7. Total exposure remains within allocation/risk.
8. Previous position has profit cushion or strong fresh confirmation exists.
9. Pullbacks are shallow.
10. No major opposite wick/rejection appears.
11. No failed flame or lockout active.

Block add-ons when:

1. Flame is Stretched, Weakening, Exhausted, Failed, or Reversal risk.
2. Price is near target/danger zone.
3. Candle bodies are shrinking.
4. Opposite wick/rejection appears.
5. Spread widens.
6. Bridge/EA heartbeat is stale.
7. User already has too many entries.
8. Total basket exposure is too high.
9. Floating profit is shrinking fast.
10. User is averaging into a losing scalp without confirmation.

Add-on styles:

1. Flame Add-On = add when momentum continues strongly.
2. Pullback Add-On = add when shallow pullback rejects.
3. Breakout Add-On = add after next micro level breaks.
4. Retest Add-On = add after broken level holds.
5. Do Not Add = flame late, stretched, choppy, or near danger.

Default max scalp entries per flame:

1. Conservative: 1–2
2. Balanced: 2–3
3. Aggressive/Owner admin: 2–4 within allocation and safety gates
4. No new add-ons after target zone is reached
5. No new add-ons during weakening/exhaustion

Do not increase size late in the flame.
Do not recommend full-size add-ons after stretch.
Entry 1 can be normal scalp size.
Entry 2 can be same or smaller if continuation confirms.
Entry 3 should usually be smaller.
Entry 4 should be caution/micro only if allowed at all.

Ruby must distinguish planned scale-in from emotional averaging/revenge.

Allowed scale-in:
“Adding into confirmed momentum with profit cushion and flame still active.”

Dangerous add:
“Adding because the first trade is losing and user wants to recover.”

Profit cushion rule:

1. Prefer add-ons only when earlier entry is in profit or not under pressure.
2. If first entry is negative, require stronger confirmation for add-on.
3. Do not add into losing scalp without fresh structure confirmation.
4. Do not allow add-on if basket is under pressure and flame is not active.

Ruby warning:
“This add-on would average into a losing scalp without confirmation. That is not a valid flame add.”

SECTION 18 — FAILED FLAME LOCKOUT AND COOLDOWN

After a flame fails, Ruby must avoid repeated low-quality entries.

Failed flame signs:

1. Breakout candle fully retraced.
2. Strong opposite candle.
3. Long wick against direction.
4. Next candle cannot continue.
5. Price closes back inside range.
6. Support/resistance rejects the move.
7. Spread spike ruins setup.
8. Multiple failed breaks.

Lockout rules:

1. After failed BUY flame, block/downgrade BUY scalps for 2–3 candles unless new structure forms.
2. After failed SELL flame, block/downgrade SELL scalps for 2–3 candles unless new structure forms.
3. After two failed scalps on same symbol, require stronger confirmation.
4. In chop, reduce alert frequency.
5. Do not keep firing signals during repeated fakeouts.

Ruby wording:
“Bearish flame failed. Lockout SELL scalp suggestions for 3 candles unless a new breakdown forms.”
