Two-phase task on AI intelligence. Phase 1 is verification, Phase 2 is diagnosis-then-scope — do not build anything in Phase 2 without reporting first.
Phase 1 — Is intelligence active end-to-end (state changes approved where noted):
1. Start the market scanner (approved — owner request) and let it complete one full scan of the universe. Report per symbol: verdict, confidence, dataSource, and confirm the truth-cap engages on rendered rows — fallback-fed symbols (e.g. GBPUSD on Polygon) must render capped below broker-clean EURUSD; no SIMULATOR data anywhere.
2. SmartChartIntelligence: confirm it's active on live broker data — run it for EURUSD M15/H1 and report the actual intelligence output (signals, structure, levels) and that its inputs are the mt5_broker feed, not fallback.
3. Ruby full reads on EURUSD M15, H1, D1: capture the complete read — bias, confidence, why, levels/zones, warnings. Verify internal consistency: the stated bias must follow from the cited evidence, warnings must correspond to real conditions in the data (report any read where the narrative contradicts its own inputs).
Phase 2 — Accuracy measurement (diagnose first, build nothing yet):
4. Check what already exists for read-outcome tracking — decision receipts, AACI scoring, any reads-vs-outcome table. Report what's there and what's missing for a prediction ledger: every Ruby read persisted with {symbol, tf, timestamp, price-at-read, bias, confidence, key levels, warnings}, plus a scoring job that evaluates each read after a horizon (e.g. 20 bars): direction hit/miss, level respect, warning materialization — aggregated into hit-rate stats per timeframe and confidence tier.
5. Propose the smallest implementation that closes the gap, as a scoped follow-up. Do not implement until approved.

Two expectations to hold: first, even after the ledger exists, accuracy numbers need sample size — a few days of reads on one symbol is noise; judge it after a few weeks across the universe. Second, watch step 3's consistency check for the failure mode that actually matters with LLM-driven reads: a fluent narrative that doesn't follow from its own cited data. That's more dangerous than a wrong call, because wrong calls show up in the ledger while incoherent reasoning hides behind confident prose.
The ledger also quietly becomes ARX's most valuable feature later: "Ruby's H1 reads at Medium confidence hit 61% over the last 90 days" is a claim you can put in front of users — and the discipline of measuring it keeps Ruby honest in a way no prompt engineering can.
