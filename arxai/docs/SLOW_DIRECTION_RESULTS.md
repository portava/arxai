## Reading these results

**Short answer: no. Nothing predicts a currency's direction over the next month on today's data.** The two best-documented directional effects in markets were tested the way every other direction claim in ARX is tested, and neither holds after 2000.

**They were real once.**

- In the first half of the sample (1971–1999), 17 of the 53 pre-registered hypotheses are discoveries after false-discovery correction.
- Buying the currencies that rose last month and selling the ones that fell (one-month time-series momentum, equal weight) earned about +39 bp a month net of cost, with p = 0.000005.
- Momentum over 3, 6 and 12 months and the carry portfolio were all clearly positive too.
- This is the effect the academic literature documented in the 1990s.

**They did not last.**

- From 2000 on, none of the 17 keeps its sign at p < 0.05. Most momentum signals fall to a few bp a month, and after 2010 they are mostly negative.
- Carry comes closest. The carry portfolio still earned +13.7 bp a month (p = 0.09), and the cross-sectional carry portfolio +12.7 bp (p = 0.026). But the first missed the confirmation bar, and the second was not a first-half discovery.
- Under the rules fixed before the run, neither counts. Moving the bar now would be choosing the answer after seeing it.

**The method is not the reason.** On seeded data, the same code finds a planted monthly momentum of 0.15 and a planted carry effect on every seed, and it finds nothing on 11 driftless seeds. If the effects were still there at that size, this test would have shown them.

**What this means for ARX.**

- The F for predicting direction stands, now with evidence at every horizon: hourly sessions, intraday strategy families, chart patterns, and now monthly momentum and carry over 55 years.
- The app should keep saying it does not know which way a market will move. That statement is correct, and it is what the "honestly labelled" in the grade refers to.
- An A+ in direction prediction would need a signal that survives this test. None exists in public price and rate data.
- The remaining possibilities are private information: order flow, positioning, news faster than the market. They are outside what this repository can measure.

**Method notes.**

- Excess returns include the interest differential, using the rate known at the start of the holding month.
- A currency-month without a rate on either side is excluded from every hypothesis, which is why NOK and SEK start in 1979 and 1982.
- EUR exists only from 1999, so it barely enters the discovery half.
- Results are net of 1 bp per unit traded. The survivors table would also report 5 bp, but there are no survivors.
- Equity indices are not tested, because their publishers license the data and this repository is public.
