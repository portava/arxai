## Reading these results

**Direction is not predictable from the clock, on this data.** None of the 406 time-of-day, session and "London follows Asia" hypotheses was even significant in the first half of the year after correcting for the number of tests. Their averages kept the same sign into the second half 53% of the time, which is what a coin would do. The strongest first-half numbers (the 21:00 UTC rollover hour on several FX pairs, for example) faded or flipped in the second half. That is the signature of noise.

This is the expected answer, and it is worth having measured. Calendar and session drifts in liquid FX and index markets are small, and the published ones tend to vanish once they are known. The positive control in the test shows the method finds a real 8bp hourly drift when one is planted, so "nothing" here is a finding, not a blind spot.

**The strategy families have no edge on a year of real bars.** This holds net of costs and gross of costs.

**Mean Reversion is fixed, and it is not a money-maker.**

- **The defect:** the old rule required an extreme RSI and a tight range at the same time. Those exclude each other, so it fired 0 times on every real series.
- **The redesign:** the close sits in the outer 20% of the prior 19-bar range, RSI(14) is below 30 or above 70, and the last bar's wick is longer than its body, rejecting the extreme. Target: the range midpoint.
- **On real markets:** it now fires about 14,000 times. It wins 26% of the time at roughly a 3:1 target-to-stop.
  - Gross it is around break-even (+0.04R per trade).
  - After a one-pip-class spread it loses about 0.19R per trade, because its stops are tight relative to the spread on M15.
  - The H1 index subgroup is slightly positive after spread (+0.06R, t 0.8), which is not significant.
- **What the user sees:** calibration measures it like every other strategy (the historical bootstrap now requires it to be measured), so its real hit rate is what gets shown.

**What would help, honestly:**

- Trade less often, on higher timeframes, where spread is a smaller share of the stop. The measured H1 numbers are better than M15 across the board.
- Use the volatility forecaster (docs/VOLATILITY_FORECAST_RESULTS.md) to size and place stops, and to skip trading in the quiet hours. That is the risk-tools lane, measured on P&L next.

**Limits.**

- One year of Deriv's own feed.
- Spreads are assumed, not measured.
- Day-level samples: about 130 per half per hypothesis, so a drift under about 2bp/hour could exist and not be detected.
