## Reading these results

**The drawings are accurate.**

- Every pattern the engine names is found when it is planted, at the planted level and with the planted status.
- None is named when its defining feature is missing.
- On 362,507 real chart windows, no pattern was drawn with disordered levels, a status that contradicts the price, or a point off the chart.
- The same chart at a different price scale reads the same.

**The patterns do not predict.**

- On a year of real FX, gold and index bars, no pattern reaches its drawn target more often than a random walk would from the same entry, stop and target. That is 0 of 32 pattern-and-timeframe cells after FDR.
- The hit rates in section 5 are almost exactly p0. A double top that "works" 64% of the time is a double top whose target sits nearer than its stop, not one that knows anything.
- So a pattern on the chart is a correct description of the shape, not a forecast. That is why the pattern score only ever lowers a scanner read (`buildPatternTruthVerdict`), and why the smart chart shows no confidence percentage.

**What the suite caught.** The planted suite was run against the engine as it was before this work:

- A head-and-shoulders was named with the head level with the shoulders (200 of 200 near misses).
- A bull or bear flag was never found (0 of 200): the flag box included the break bar, so the break could never be seen. On real bars every one of 4,943 flags stayed "forming".
- A breakout-and-retest fired on a level price had left long before (200 of 200 near misses). On real bars it fired on 47% of all windows; it now fires on about 12%.

**The real-bar checks also forced these fixes:**

- **Level precision.** Levels were rounded to 4 decimals on 5-decimal FX quotes, so stop and confirmation collapsed onto one price (310 cases). They now round to the quote's own precision.
- **Status vs the drawn level.** A head-and-shoulders status was judged against the unrounded neckline, so it could disagree with the line on screen. It is now judged against the rounded line.
- **Ties.** Prices are whole ticks, so a distance often equals its ATR threshold exactly. Float noise then settled the tie differently at different price scales:
  - EURUSD and the same chart ×100 picked different breakout levels on about 1 window in 4;
  - pin bars and flags appeared at one scale and not the other.
  
  Thresholds now carry a slack far below one tick, and midpoints round half-up the same way at every scale.
- **Liquidity sweep target.** It was drawn at the window's most extreme swing, several times further away than the library's spec ("the nearest prior swing"). Measured that way, sweeps reached their target LESS often than chance (all four cells significant). With the spec's target they are at chance, like every other pattern.

**Method notes.**

- Entries are counted once per pattern instance, with at most one open per series and pattern.
- A bar that touches both target and stop is excluded, because it cannot say which came first.
- Outcomes are gross of spread. A pattern that cannot beat p0 before costs cannot beat it after.
