## Reading these results

**These tools cut losses. They do not create an edge.** Every strategy is still negative after spread, with or without them. Nothing here should be read as "now it makes money".

**The cost gate does most of the work.**

- More than half of all calls risk so little (a tight ATR stop on M15) that a one-pip-class spread eats over 10% of the risk before price moves.
- Refusing those calls halves the average loss per trade, from −0.175R to −0.09R, and cuts the total loss by about 80%.
- The gross columns show it does this ONLY by avoiding cost. The trades it keeps are no better before costs; if anything they are slightly worse (−0.03R vs +0.01R gross).
- That is what you would expect from strategies with no directional edge: the only lever left is not paying for trades that cannot cover their spread.

**The quiet-hours gate helps a little** (−0.153R vs −0.175R per trade). Gross, the difference is not significant (p 0.17). Quiet hours mostly mean tight stops, which again means cost.

**Vol-scaled risk smooths the ride, slightly.** Sizing each trade by the forecaster's expected volatility (smaller when the next hours look wild, larger when calm, clamped to 0.5–2×) lowers the per-trade spread of outcomes by 13% and the summed drawdowns by 15%. The per-trade Sharpe is unchanged, and it is still negative.

**Measured expectancy per strategy** is the table above. These are the real numbers to show next to a strategy, not a model probability.

**A defect found along the way: Break of Structure could emit an impossible trade.**

- **When it happened:** after a breakout, if the price had already closed back through the opposite swing, the rule still fired.
- **What it emitted:** a BUY whose stop sat above its entry and whose target sat below it.
- **How often:** 98 of 5,457 calls on real H1 bars.
- **The fix:** the setup is now void once price is past its own stop level. A test now checks stop/target geometry for every call from every strategy on the real data.

**Limits.**

- Spreads are assumed, not measured. Real broker spreads widen at the rollover and on news, which would make the cost gate refuse more.
- One year of data.
- The gates are fixed rules, not tuned: 10% and the 20th percentile were chosen before looking, and other values were not tried.
- Trades are graded first-touch within 60 bars, as calibration does.

**How the tools are exposed.** They are pure functions in `lib/risk/src/tradeGates.ts`: `roundTripCostR`, `costGate`, `quietGate` and `sigmaExpectedFromVariance` (the sigma that `volTargetBaseFrac` takes). Each can only refuse or shrink a trade. None places, enlarges or flips one.
