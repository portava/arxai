# Native MT5 read-only market transport

The approved universe contains 43 instruments and 21 MT5 chart periods: 903 series. Runtime inventory resolution uses exact registry aliases. DXY binds to the broker CFD `DXYUSD` (`US Dollar Index`), not ICE spot or a USD basket. V75 remains `Volatility 75 Index`; the separate `Volatility 75 (1s) Index` chart drives scheduling only.

The exact deployed MQL sources are in `../runtime/`. The EA maintains the read-only heartbeat and economic calendar. It creates/reuses a tagged always-open synthetic chart and attaches `ArxHistoryCollector`; it does not wait on CopyBuffer, read candles, poll commands, or run legacy account/position producers in its timer. All seven trading arms remain immutable false and Algo Trading remains off. Calendar collection refreshes the existing safety heartbeat after provider reads and immediately before dispatch; the calendar relay's 30-second safety requirement is unchanged.

The chart-attached indicator owns quotes, native closed candles, and inventory. Quotes and M1 closed tips target 2-second scheduling. The other 20 periods rotate through two groups per timer invocation. Seed work is bounded to 12 series and 150 ms between calls per invocation. Backfill has its own 100 ms slice, with at most 24 selections, so an expensive seed call cannot eliminate backfill service. These limits are checked between native calls, not hard preemption deadlines. MT5 indicator CopyRates initiates asynchronous history building instead of waiting on cold downloads, unlike EA CopyRates. [Official CopyRates documentation](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/series/copyrates).

A fixed 16-slot retry queue gives incomplete native backfill reads at most two prompt retries, with a one-second hydration delay. It alternates retries with regular cursor slots, preserving eventual service for all 903 series even when retries keep failing. A completed page is not recollected for the same server cursor within 30 seconds; later refreshes still perform new native reads. All page sizes, source timestamps, broker boundary proofs, identity and read-only guards remain enforced. The queue header is part of the collector deployment and is mirrored under `runtime/`.

Every full-series collection first requests 200 actual closed bars. A short local cache is never treated as proof of the broker's historical limit: partial fallbacks preserve full-request count/error, observed count, local first-history date, and server first-history date. `brokerLimited` is asserted only when a partial snapshot reaches the reported broker history boundary. Full-seed forwarding rejects short local caches until the reported broker boundary is reached; two-bar updates use a separate contract. This prevents transient hydration boundaries from being promoted as complete durable history. Pending 4401 history errors remain unavailable and are retried. No bars or values are padded or aggregated locally.

The Python contract validates account/server, read-only guards, current clock evidence, exact native symbols, five-second quote freshness, native bid/ask, closed state, OHLC, and tick volume. Current broker UTC offset is observed and checked; an offset change fails validation. Historical timestamps remain broker-server coordinates, without an invented historical timezone. Monthly closeTime is the first day of the next calendar month, including leap February and December rollover. All other periods use their real MT5 interval.

Transport only sends authenticated TICK packets to `/api/bridge/v2/ingest` and native closed-bar batches to `/api/mt5/candles/ingest` through fixed loopback `127.0.0.1:18765`. A credential-free GET of the same API's public `/api/healthz` supplies uptime for conservative receipt restoration. Redirects and environment proxies are disabled. No command, account, position, or execution routes are allowed.

The three history workers share a bounded seed/backfill scheduler. When both classes have eligible work, each gets at least one worker as slots become free; the third follows the oldest attempt. An idle class lends its slot. Within each class, oldest attempt comes before unresolved-depth preference, so continuously refreshed failed seeds cannot indefinitely outrank a repaired backfill or an older seeded series. Selection accounts for workers already occupied, including when only one slot becomes free. The dispatcher runs independently of quote HTTP latency and admits at most three jobs, with no unbounded executor backlog. Files last written more than 120 seconds ago are excluded before worker admission. Fair scheduling removes class starvation but does not promise that every page will meet its freshness deadline under sustained overload; an expired page still requires a new native read. File age is only a scheduling filter: a recent file still needs its original observation timestamp, identity and all other validations to pass. Status counts distinguish recent candidates from old skipped files. Transient transport failures, HTTP 429 and HTTP 5xx retry an unchanged file after three seconds; the worker reruns all original identity and freshness checks, and permanent validation or authorization failures wait for a new snapshot. Historical depth growth triggers an immediate reseed even when the newest bar is unchanged. Three dedicated M1 workers and three other-timeframe workers forward new 1–2 bar batches. Each lane admits at most six jobs, ordered fairly so fresh snapshots cannot sit in an unbounded stale queue. When the native closed tip is unchanged, a newly collected snapshot re-observes the same bars after 90 seconds; original bar timestamps, OHLC, volume, identity, and the real observation timestamp are preserved. Snapshots older than 15 seconds cannot enter this lane. This keeps provider liveness within its existing five-minute expiry without calling old candles newly formed. The quote lane is independent. Shutdown cancels pending work and lets only bounded in-flight requests finish.

After relay restart, a previous full-seed receipt is restored only when public API uptime proves acceptance occurred in the current server boot, beyond a conservative five-second boundary margin. This restores knowledge of data already accepted by the same running server; it never restamps or resends an old observation. Unproven receipts require fresh ingestion. Full histories refresh periodically and all source freshness checks remain enforced.

`relay-status.json` distinguishes accepted full seeds, fast updates, actual depth, and failures. `accepted-evidence/` keeps actual accepted payloads for exact chart/quote comparisons. Logs rotate; evidence and state are excluded from version control. No tokens are written to these artifacts. Measured bounded restart: old relay exited 1.028 s after SIGTERM; supervised replacement accepted 43 quotes after 10.447 s, without restarting MT5/API/calendar. Initial cold-load M1 acceptance reached 17.60 s and 12.95 s at two boundaries, then 5.76 s after backlog settled; these are recorded observations, not a guaranteed latency claim. Freshness failures remain visible.

Closed native history can change while MT5 hydrates earlier underlying data. Weekly/monthly conflicts discovered during verification are archived with both versions; the API must retain its immutable closed-bar guard or apply a separately audited correction. A stable current snapshot alone does not establish the cause of an older conflict.


### Native closed-to-forming observation metadata

`ArxNativeSeamObservation.mqh` reads native shift 1 and shift 0 together with `CopyRates(..., 0, 2, ...)`. It adds a separate `nativeSeam` sibling to the fast snapshot; the closed candle array and candle-ingest payload remain unchanged. Each pair carries its exact canonical/native symbol, timeframe, native previous/current OHLC and open times, UTC collection time, raw broker clock and measured offset. The account, server, connected terminal and read-only/Algo-OFF guards remain mandatory.

A separate bounded two-worker relay lane forwards at most 50 observations per request only to `/api/bridge/v2/native-seams`, using the existing authenticated transport. It accepts only fresh, actually reobserved terminal snapshots and preserves raw second-level clock jitter without claiming a historical timezone. This metadata never travels through TICK/CANDLE ingestion or any order/account/command route.

`native-seam-evidence/` retains the exact request, ordered identity-bound API receipt and raw source snapshot hash. An accepted receipt proves observation storage only: the API must independently match the writer, native symbol, previous closed timestamp and all four OHLC values to the served history before treating a seam as measured. Forming OHLC never enters closed-candle analysis. Session gaps remain explicit; calendar month boundaries use calendar arithmetic.

The native metadata lane forwards a newly observed forming-boundary or native-symbol change immediately within its bounded fair queue; otherwise it renews at a20-second cadence. It never predicts a rollover from UTC or changes an old payload's timestamp.

### Bounded XAU native history warmup

The guarded startup hook maintains a dedicated plain XAUUSD M1 chart and attaches `ArxXauHistoryDiagnostic` under the same immutable read-only guards. The indicator performs at most five minutes of same-symbol native history reads into a separate `diagnostic-xau-*.json` spool. Those filenames are excluded from all forwarding lanes. It then stops its timer/reads while remaining attached. The main collector on the always-open synthetic chart continues supplying every quote and candle.

This addresses the observed native history-build behavior: foreign-symbol XAU reads repeatedly returned4401 although quotes were fresh. A plain chart alone did not resolve the other periods; same-symbol native reads initialized all21periods, after which the main collector supplied903/903 pairs. The local before/after and post-expiry traces retain the cold failures and verify continued actual reads without inventing or substituting history. No diagnostic prices become analytical input. On terminal startup the bounded warmup repeats; normal freshness and missing-data guards remain in effect throughout.
